## Discovered, not detected

Ask a head of maintenance how the last water damage was found. The answer is almost never "the system told us". It is a tenant on the phone, a stain spreading across a ceiling, a puddle on the basement floor on Monday morning. The leak was discovered, not detected. By the time anyone knew, it had been running for days, sometimes weeks.

That delay is the whole problem. Water damage is not expensive because water is expensive. It is expensive because of time: every extra day soaks subfloors, drywall and the unit below.

## The five-visit problem

Once a leak is known, finding it is the next job. A typical search in a multi-storey building: a first visit to confirm the damage, a second to open a wall where the stain is, a third when the water turns out to come from a floor higher up, a fourth with a plumber, a fifth to repair. Up to five site visits, and a wall opened each time, for one leak.

The reason is simple. Water travels. The stain is rarely under the source. Without a view of where flow is abnormal, the search follows the water backwards, one opened wall at a time.

## Why meters do not see it

Most buildings already have water meters, some of them smart. A meter counts consumption per unit or per building. A hidden leak in a riser or a supply line does not belong to a unit, so it shows up in no single meter. On the building total it hides inside normal consumption, which swings by far more from day to day than a leak adds.

Leaks hide in the noise of normal use. That is why counting water is not the same as watching it.

## What a live view changes

Occupancy has a dashboard. Energy has a dashboard. HVAC has a dashboard. Water is the one building system without a live view.

A live view means a flow reading per riser every few minutes, and a model that knows what normal looks like for that riser: the night baseline, the morning peak, weekday against weekend. A deviation is a flow pattern that normal does not explain. The maintenance team gets an alert that names the riser and the floors to check first, instead of a stain to work backwards from.

It changes the search from five visits to one targeted one. It does not change the repair. Plumbers still fix pipes. The system only tells them where to start.

## What to do this week

Three things a portfolio can do before any sensor is installed.

1. **Pull the last five water claims** and note where each leak was found and how long it had run. The pattern is usually the same: shared network, weeks, found by a tenant.
2. **Pick the building** with the most units on the fewest risers. It is the fastest proof of a live view.
3. **Send the plumbing plan.** The sensor positions and the digital twin are planned from it before anyone is on site.
