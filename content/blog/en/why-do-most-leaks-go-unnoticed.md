## Three reasons leaks stay hidden

**They start where nobody looks.** Risers run in shafts. Supply lines run above ceilings and under floors. The parts of a building that carry the most water are the parts no one walks past.

**They are small at first.** A weeping joint adds a trickle to a riser that moves thousands of litres a day. On the building's main meter, that trickle is a rounding error. Normal consumption swings by far more between a Tuesday and a Sunday than the leak adds.

**Nothing is watching.** Occupancy has a dashboard. Energy has a dashboard. HVAC has a dashboard. Water, in most buildings, waits for a phone call.

## What the first signs look like

By the time a leak is discovered, it has usually announced itself three times. First as a stain that someone assumed was old. Then as a smell in a stairwell. Then as water on a basement floor on a Monday morning. Each sign is weeks apart, and each is downstream of the source. The search that follows works backwards from the last sign, one opened wall at a time.

## What a live view changes

A clamp-on sensor on the riser reads the flow every 10 minutes. A model learns that riser's normal: the night baseline, the morning peak, the weekly rhythm. The night baseline is the tell. When every tap is closed and the flow still does not return to its usual level, water is moving where it should not. The model flags it, and the digital twin names the riser and the floors to check first.

## The difference in practice

Without a live view, the leak is discovered. With one, it is detected: by the pattern, at night, before a tenant sees anything. The team gets a riser and a floor instead of a stain, and the search starts in the right place.

## Where to look if you are looking by hand

Until a building has a live view, three habits catch more than random inspection.

1. **Check the main meter at night.** Read it at 11 p.m. and 5 a.m. on a night with no known use. A difference that is far above the building's usual night use is worth a riser check.
2. **Walk the shafts, not the corridors.** Leaks start where the risers are. A shaft door that is always closed is where a stain goes unseen longest.
3. **Log the stains.** A stain that reappears after painting is a source, not a one-off. Logging where and when turns three unrelated complaints into one riser.

A live view does all three automatically, every 10 minutes, on every riser, which is the point.
