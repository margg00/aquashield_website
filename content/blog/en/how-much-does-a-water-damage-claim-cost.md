## The number

NAIOP puts the average US commercial water damage claim at about USD 89,000. That is one claim, one building, one event. Across a portfolio, the question is not whether a claim happens but how many, and how big each one gets before it is stopped.

## What the money is spent on

A water claim is rarely about the pipe. The pipe repair is a plumber and a part. The money goes to what the water touched while it ran:

- Subfloors and screeds that have to dry or be replaced.
- Drywall and insulation that have to come out.
- Neighbouring units below and beside the source.
- Business interruption or loss of rent while the work is done.
- The search itself: site visits and walls opened to find the source.

Every item on that list grows with time. A leak stopped on day one touches a subfloor. The same leak stopped on day twenty has reached two more units.

## Time is the lever

That is why loss duration matters more than almost anything else. The water flow rate of a weeping joint is small. What makes it expensive is the number of days it runs unseen. In a building without a live view of its water, that number is set by chance: when a tenant notices, when a stain appears, when someone walks past the right spot in the basement.

Monitoring changes the variable. A model that has learned the building's normal flow sees a night baseline that no longer returns to its usual level, and the digital twin places it on a riser. The team checks that riser in days, not weeks.

## For owners and insurers

For an owner, the comparison is simple: the monitoring of a building against a fraction of one claim at that building. For an insurer, the lever is the same but the view is the book: shorter loss duration across many buildings means lower severity across many claims, and live building data gives a risk signal that underwriting does not have today.

## A worked example without the water

Take a building where a riser joint begins to weep on a Monday.

- **Found on day 3** by a flow deviation at night: a plumber, a part, one dried subfloor section behind an access panel.
- **Found on day 24** by a stain in the flat below: two flats with wet drywall, a subfloor to replace, a ceiling to open and close, a tenant moved out for the works, and three site visits to find the source before any of it.

Same joint, same trickle. The difference between the two bills is the time the water ran, and that is the only variable monitoring changes. It does not make pipes stronger. It makes the clock shorter.
